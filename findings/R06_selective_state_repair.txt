R06 — Selective contraction can stabilize a known nuisance geometry

EVIDENCE STATUS: FIVE-SEED TOY SUCCESS; LANGUAGE TRANSFER FAILED

A geometry-aware finite-difference objective greatly improved long rollouts on a controlled dynamics task.

THE IDEA
Suppress state errors in directions known to be harmless while retaining
directions that carry the task's information. Train a shared transition with
ordinary prediction loss plus a perturbation-size-normalized secant loss.

WHAT SUCCEEDED
The task tracked two rotating circles: phase carried information, radial
motion was nuisance, and exact nuisance geometry was supplied in training.
Every neural model had 4,868 parameters. Five fresh confirmation seeds gave
64-step autonomous-rollout MSE:
  RMS normalization:             0.406534
  Random-direction regularizer:  0.142391
  Four-step BPTT:                0.227320
  Eight-step BPTT, half batch:    0.147260
  Selective repair:              0.029152
  Exact geometry oracle:         0.017158
Selective repair reduced MSE by 80.20% relative to eight-step BPTT and won
every paired seed. One-step MSE worsened 5.19%, within the 25% gate.
Estimated matrix work was about 4.2% higher, not exactly equal wall compute.

THE IMPORTANT FAILED TRANSFER
On Tiny Shakespeare, teacher-verified low-sensitivity directions did not make
the recurrent student better: test CE was 1.908269 versus ordinary recurrent
distillation 1.908258. A stronger regularizer reduced local sensitivity 92.07%
but worsened validation CE 1.40%. Improving an internal stability metric did
not improve language prediction. The dense distilled student was stronger.

HOW TO BUILD ON IT
Use this objective only when nuisance geometry and long-horizon instability
are defensible task properties. Compare random-direction and longer-BPTT
controls, track clean one-step loss, and evaluate full rollouts. Low teacher
sensitivity alone does not justify assuming a direction is disposable.

ORIGINALITY
The successful toy recipe and failed language transfer are the contribution
candidate. Weight reuse, distillation, Jacobian constraints and denoising
are established; no new universal learning principle was verified.
