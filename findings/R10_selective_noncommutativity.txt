R10 — Open richer operators where observed action order matters

EVIDENCE STATUS: EXPLORATORY TWO-WORLD SUCCESS AGAINST MATCHED RANDOM OPENING

Observed commutation violations selected useful extra matrix entries better than random selection.

THE IDEA
Begin with diagonal action matrices, which commute. Inspect complete two-action
squares in training data. If a then b and b then a reach different observed
endpoints, allow off-diagonal entries for the involved actions before fitting.
Keep the simpler representation for actions without observed violations.

WHAT SUCCEEDED
Two structured noncommuting worlds; 2,000 updates per arm. Adaptive opening and
same-count random opening each had 624 active matrix entries, versus 96 for
diagonal and 1,152 for fully dense. Adaptive opening scored 66.24% on held-out
edges versus random 41.88% and dense 33.25%. Both-members-correct on
opposite-order pairs was 42.19% versus random 19.53% and dense 34.96%.
The trigger selected actions 4-7 from observed training squares; those indices
were outputs of the test, not supplied hidden action labels.

IMPORTANT COUNTEREVIDENCE
Fixed diagonal scored 90.61% on held-out edges but 0% on opposite-order pairs.
Adaptive opening therefore did not dominate every metric. An engineered
empirical commuting-square closure control scored 100% on both metrics.
All state identities were already known; these are missing-edge and composition
tests, not generalization to unseen identities. Only two worlds were tested.

HOW TO BUILD ON IT
Use falsified structural assumptions to choose where capacity is added, and
compare against equally sized random opening. Keep both ordinary prediction
and the exact counterfactual that triggered expansion in the evaluation.
The trigger here was programmed, not learned, and ran before fitting; online
architecture growth, context-dependent rules and irreversible actions remain
untested. An analogous selective LoRA allocation would be a new experiment.

ORIGINALITY
The training-square trigger combined with selective matrix opening is an
unresolved originality candidate. Noncommutative relation matrices and
algebra-compatible representations have close prior work.
