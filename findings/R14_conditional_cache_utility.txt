R14 — Cache-removal labels change after earlier evictions

EVIDENCE STATUS: SYNTHETIC TRAINING-TARGET SUCCESS; NO REAL KV OR VRAM RESULT

A conditional removal target preserved redundant evidence that static full-cache ablations missed.

THE IDEA
A block may be dispensable while its duplicate remains and essential after
that duplicate is evicted. Labels computed only against the full cache can
therefore be inappropriate for a sequence of removal decisions.

WHAT SUCCEEDED
The toy had eight logical blocks: two copies of evidence A, two of B and four
distractors. The fixed reader needed both A and B for A XOR B. At a two-block
cap, a selector trained on current-state removal harm retained both evidence
groups in 1,000/1,000 held-out episodes. Static full-cache labels, attention
and recency retained the required pair in 0/1,000; random retained it in
138/1,000. The matched-row static-label replay control also failed, isolating
the changed labels within this toy. An exact group-count program also got
1,000/1,000, without learning.

COSTS THAT MATTER
Conditional supervision needed 8,448 ablations versus 2,048 static ablations,
4.125x as many. Sequential eviction required 33 tiny scoring calls per item.
Each learned selector had ten parameters, and group/category information was
supplied. This was a standard-library CPU simulation, not real LLM attention
or K/V tensors. A 75% block-count reduction is not a measured VRAM saving.

HOW TO BUILD ON IT
Test redundancy-aware removal targets on actual model state before adopting
a learned cache policy. Include a simple group-count/diversity baseline,
charge label generation and selection overhead, and measure whole-answer
quality. The archive did not establish that the sequential policy scales.

ORIGINALITY
The controlled label failure is the useful observation. State-dependent
marginal utility and learned cache retention are established directions;
global novelty and a practical LLM advantage are unestablished.
