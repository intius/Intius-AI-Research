R04 — Evidence cross terms helped a small composition model

EVIDENCE STATUS: THREE-INITIALIZATION SYNTHETIC ABLATION SUCCESS

Squaring summed responses outperformed summing squared responses at the same parameter count.

THE IDEA
Allow evidence contributions to interact before converting them to nonnegative
scores. The matched ablation changes sum_j a_j^2 into (sum_j a_j)^2. The latter
contains pairwise cross terms. This is ordinary real-valued computation.

WHAT SUCCEEDED
On a fresh synthetic composition set, three paired initializations averaged
33.54% for the coherent model versus 27.47% for the incoherent control:
+6.07 percentage points. Per-seed comparisons were 33.64/28.52,
33.84/25.98 and 33.15/27.93. Both models had 4,520 trained real values and
18,080 parameter bytes, and began each paired run with identical weights.
Context mixing, anchoring, supplied execution, batches and update budget were
retained. The three models shared the same test worlds.

LIMITS
Absolute accuracy remained low. Strong symbolic/library controls were much
better. Complex-number and tensor-chain variants did not establish a useful
advantage. No quantum hardware, quantum speedup, natural-language advantage
or general-reasoning claim follows. The whole campaign's 414.1 MiB process
peak is not the individual coherent model's footprint.

HOW TO BUILD ON IT
Test cross terms as a narrowly specified scoring choice with an exact
parameter-matched control. Preserve the sign and normalization conventions
from the implementation. Compare against ordinary quadratic/bilinear features,
and run stronger converged neural controls before claiming an architecture
improvement. An application inside attention or a LoRA is untested here.

ORIGINALITY
The matched local observation is worth retaining. Squared-amplitude models
and multiplicative interactions are known; the report's quantum terminology
does not establish a new physical or computational principle.
