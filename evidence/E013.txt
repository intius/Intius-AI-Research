0001 | # What the experimental computations do
0002 | 
0003 | All computation in this round is classical. The task supplies examples of an
0004 | unknown function, and the structured models predict its missing table entries.
0005 | A supplied interpreter then composes predicted functions. Learning the table
0006 | and learning an interpreter are different difficulties; direct sequence models
0007 | must do more of the latter themselves.
0008 | 
0009 | ## Interference without quadratic storage
0010 | 
0011 | For a proposed input/output pair (x,y), each observed pair (s,t) retrieves a
0012 | learned kernel response k(x-s,y-t), with differences taken modulo eleven.
0013 | The original relation model averages responses, mixes four channels, and
0014 | applies softmax. The real-amplitude variant squares each channel's average
0015 | before combining channels and normalizing. Squaring gives
0016 | 
0017 |     (sum_i k_i)^2 = sum_i k_i^2 + 2 sum_{i<j} k_i k_j.
0018 | 
0019 | The cross terms couple the evidence pairs without storing separate parameters
0020 | for every pair. Their benefit is a testable representational hypothesis; it is
0021 | not new physics or an exponential computational resource. The complex variant
0022 | uses two real coordinates and square magnitude. The incoherent ablation sums
0023 | squared responses before pooling, removing cross terms. The gate variant
0024 | learns a context-dependent blend of the two intensities.
0025 | 
0026 | The original relation and real-amplitude models each have 4,520 learned real
0027 | values. Complex coherent and complex incoherent models each have 5,004.
0028 | The gate adds parameters (8,005 total); its comparison is not parameter-matched.
0029 | The selected real candidate's originally designated incoherent control has two
0030 | coordinates rather than one. If that candidate wins development selection,
0031 | an exact one-coordinate ablation is needed to isolate interference itself.
0032 | 
0033 | ## Tensor chains
0034 | 
0035 | An operator's eleven evidence tokens select small matrices. Their ordered
0036 | product carries an eight-dimensional latent state, normalized during the
0037 | contraction. A readout predicts the eleven-by-eleven transition table. The
0038 | softmax version, real Born version and complex Born version compare readout
0039 | and representation choices. The complex version has substantially more real
0040 | numbers; equal bond dimension is not equal parameter count.
0041 | 
0042 | ## Reusing neural parameters
0043 | 
0044 | Shared refinement repeatedly applies one attention block to evidence, either
0045 | with or without feedback from its errors on observed transitions. Ridge and
0046 | delta fast-weight models fit a small temporary associative memory from the
0047 | current examples; this costs inference work and temporary memory. Generated
0048 | low-rank operators construct transition factors from evidence. The routed
0049 | expert model currently evaluates all four experts and selects one; its code
0050 | does not demonstrate reduced sparse-compute cost.
0051 | 
0052 | The direct sequence controls use a small causal Transformer, four applications
0053 | of a shared block, or four learned scratch tokens. They operate on synthetic
0054 | tokens and are not natural-language LLMs. Repeating a block increases compute;
0055 | extra depth is not free capacity.
0056 | 
0057 | ## Symbolic methods
0058 | 
0059 | Finite-field interpolation infers a polynomial from current observations.
0060 | Five distinct examples determine every degree-at-most-four polynomial over
0061 | the supplied field. A high cubic or quartic score therefore credits this known
0062 | mathematical prior. Segmented inference searches simple piecewise affine rules.
0063 | Library repair uses a symbolic prediction when no learned rule matches all
0064 | examples; mixture variants compare predictions on held-out demonstrations.
0065 | Joint inference keeps one hypothesis for an operator across repeated calls,
0066 | enumerating small joint spaces and sampling larger ones.
0067 | 
0068 | These are bounded inference algorithms. Low learned-state size excludes the
0069 | algorithms, mathematical priors, inputs and runtime memory. Success here does
0070 | not establish compression of broad language knowledge or coding ability.
