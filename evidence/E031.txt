It stopped before public/IFEval development or sealed final.

0107 | ## Memory deployment probe
0108 | 
0109 | The standard NF4 4-bit load (bitsandbytes 0.50.2, double quantization, FP16 compute) worked with the selected adapter. Loaded resident allocation was 3.74 GB. On the same generated 24-case holdout, peak allocated VRAM fell from 8.69 GB FP16 to 3.92 GB NF4 (**54.95% less**), but strict accuracy fell from 15/24 to 12/24 and generation time rose from 114.5 to 158.8 seconds. All three discordant answers favored FP16; exact paired p = 0.25, so the tiny sample does not prove a general degradation. NF4 nevertheless missed the predeclared limit of at most two lost answers. Its remaining public run was stopped after three rows, and FP16 stays selected. The stopped pilot is locally archived as `GODLY3B_nf4_pilot.zip`, SHA-256 `c866f3e014429bbe60a4426f157ab63f82e3bc34da867bd732039d200d642762`, with all four recorded member hashes and ZIP CRC verified. Details are in `NF4_ANALYSIS.json`.
0110 | 
0111 | An 8-bit load with the same adapter retained the **exact same strict correctness on all 24 paired held-out cases** (15/24 each, no discordant cases) and reduced peak allocated VRAM from 8.69 to 5.45 GB (**37.30% less**). It took 295.0 seconds versus FP16's 114.5 seconds (2.576× as long) and failed the frozen latency limit. The public subset was therefore unrun in 8-bit. PEFT 0.13.2 required a recorded, inference-only compatibility field on 154 bitsandbytes 0.50.2 layers before LoRA attachment; no weights, prompts or scoring changed. The 8-bit profile is a possible low-memory tradeoff, **not** the selected default or a fully validated substitute. Its raw pilot archive `GODLY3B_int8_pilot.zip` is SHA-256 `58c9e32da4175faaeeade29960fd7d01bd225802e15ce8c5052b4579adf55bf4`, with all three recorded member hashes and ZIP CRC verified. Details are in `INT8_ANALYSIS.json`.
