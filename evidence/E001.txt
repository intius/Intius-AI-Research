0013 | EXACT COMPUTATION REBUILT
0014 | For q_i(v)=alpha_i*u(v)+s_i(v), a block-shared correction exp(b_g(v)) has
0015 | Z_i=alpha_i*sum_v u(v)*exp(b_g(v))+sum_sparse s_i(v)*exp(b_g(v)).
0016 | Independent float64 loss/gradient comparisons and finite differences passed.
0017 | Against a prepacked dense batched-matrix control on one CPU thread:
0018 |  16 sparse entries/token: 5.891x forward+backward component speedup.
0019 |  256 sparse entries/token: 4.021x.
0020 | Adding query-specific pointer mass retained exact gradients and yielded
0021 | 5.010x and 3.284x respectively. These exclude prior preparation and the neural
0022 | backbone/optimizer. They are component measurements, not full training savings.
0023 | 
0024 | THE QUERY PROBLEM AND ITS FIX
0025 | One correction shared by a block cannot independently respond to every local
0026 | query when the cheap prior gives those queries the same distribution.
0027 | I tested query-dependent sparse/copy distributions, then token-specific sparse
0028 | logit corrections that refine the existing prior directly. For a sparse tilt d:
0029 |  Z_i=Z_static_i+sum_(v in S_i) q_i(v)*exp(b_g(v))*expm1(d_i(v)).
0030 | S_i is selected from the prior using observed context, never the target label.
0031 | Local/global signals are normalized separately. A bounded tilt limits numerical
0032 | cancellation. The expensive core still operates on completed previous patches;
0033 | causal checks show no access to future tokens in the current patch.
0034 | 
0035 | This avoids making an initially weak new expert compete with a strong prior
0036 | behind a gate that closes before the expert learns. The evidence supports the
0037 | tested combination of local normalization and prior-relative corrections;
0038 | it does not establish that every improvement comes from one isolated change.

0070 | QUALITY LIMITS
0071 | The earlier strong statistical model scored 1.708708 on the pilot's identical
0072 | validation sample; the new neural tilt scored 2.003369 there. Beating a weak
0073 | count prior or one neural correction baseline is not sufficient by itself.
0074 | Full BLiMP diagnostic, 67,000 pairs, 128-byte contexts with causal padding:
0075 |   dense          52.227%
0076 |   tilt           52.691%
0077 | These results do not establish high-quality grammar. Anaphor agreement in
0078 | the selected diagnostic seed fell from 59.35% for dense to 45.70% for tilt,
0079 | despite the small aggregate accuracy increase. Capabilities are not uniformly
0080 | preserved. Thirty-two evaluated sentences exceeded the 128-byte window and
0081 | were scored with documented resets. No BLiMP labels trained these models.

0083 | WHY THIS IS NOT THE REQUESTED BREAKTHROUGH
0084 | The normalized-loss trick and query fix are useful building blocks. Exponential
0085 | tilting, neural corrections, copy/pointer mechanisms and hierarchical computation
0086 | have prior art; no exhaustive novelty result is claimed. With a fixed per-example
0087 | cost model, running a component every K tokens alone cannot give more than Kx
0088 | savings even if all other work vanished. A 16-token block is therefore not by
0089 | itself a 100x fixed-work training solution. Different sample efficiency could
0090 | change time-to-quality, but must be measured and was not established here.
0091 | No 1B model, frontier benchmark win, GPU-scale result or broad reasoning advance
0092 | was demonstrated. The original 100x-and-size study's failed quality gate remains
0093 | failed and is separate from the modest neural-correction success reported here.
