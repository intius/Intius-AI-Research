0001 | 01 — PROOF-DEPENDENCY REVISION PROPAGATION: CPU PRIMITIVE SCREEN
0002 | Date: 2026-09-23
0003 | Status: INCONCLUSIVE AS AN AI ADVANCE; bounded exact mechanism evidence.
0004 | 
0005 | QUESTION
0006 | Can a persistent belief store repair derived facts after an edit by visiting
0007 | only conclusions that depend on the changed fact, while matching a strong exact
0008 | recomputation control?
0009 | 
0010 | MECHANISM TESTED
0011 | For supplied acyclic unary and binary rules, each fact has a current base
0012 | assertion and derived truth value. Each rule is a separate support. The
0013 | candidate builds a reverse index from premise to dependent rule, traverses the
0014 | transitive dependents after a base write, and re-evaluates those nodes in
0015 | topological order. Independent rules preserve alternative support. This is an
0016 | incremental dependency-propagation screen; it is not a natural-language writer,
0017 | learned belief system, or full evidence-provenance implementation.
0018 | 
0019 | OBSERVED — PRIMARY RESULTS
0020 | Three CPU runs used seeds 1701, 1702 and 1703. Each graph contained 20,000
0021 | propositions, 16 scopes, about 26,500 unary/binary rules, and 135 sparse
0022 | cross-scope rules. Each received 60 scheduled source writes. Every update was
0023 | followed by comparison of all 20,000 candidate truth values against full
0024 | recomputation.
0025 | 
0026 | - Exact parity: all three seeds matched after every scheduled write; zero stale
0027 |   propositions were observed across 180 candidate/control state comparisons.
0028 | - Actual changed writes: 58, 57 and 58 per seed. A raw-input audit found 2, 3
0029 |   and 2 repeated idempotent writes respectively. This is a protocol deviation
0030 |   from the frozen prediction of 60 changed claims per seed; the result is
0031 |   treated as a qualified screen, not a clean confirmatory pass.
0032 | - Median affected graph fraction by seed: 0.0175%, 0.0100%, 0.0100%.
0033 |   Median across seeds: 0.0100%.
0034 | - P95 affected fraction by seed: 69.165%, 64.860%, 53.120%. Some cross-scope
0035 |   corrections were broad and touched most of the graph.
0036 | - A post-hoc structural audit of preserved graph/event files found a bimodal
0037 |   workload: 67/180 writes (37.2%) touched only the edited source, 41 touched
0038 |   2–10 nodes, 23 touched 1,001–5,000 nodes, and 48 (26.7%) touched more than
0039 |   5,000 nodes. Maximum impact was 16,217/20,000 nodes (81.1%). 71/180 writes
0040 |   (39.4%) traversed at least one of the 135 cross-scope rules.
0041 | - The candidate scanned 831,023 reverse-index premise incidences in total
0042 |   across the 180 scheduled writes, including 4,509 cross-scope incidences.
0043 |   This traversal is real candidate work. The 10,284.5x statistic below counts
0044 |   support-rule evaluations only and omits these index-edge scans; it is not a
0045 |   total-work reduction.
0046 | - Median support-rule checks for full recomputation: 20,942; 20,569; 20,919.
0047 |   For the candidate: 4; 2; 1.5. The median per-seed reduction factors were
0048 |   5,235.5x, 10,284.5x and 13,946x; the median of these was 10,284.5x. These
0049 |   counts include work on the synthetic graph only, not language parsing or
0050 |   answer generation. Event-level latency arrays were not preserved, so
0051 |   per-edit latency bins and maximum latency are unavailable; only per-seed
0052 |   median and P95 times below were retained.
0053 | - Median update latency: full recomputation 10.17–10.33 ms; candidate
0054 |   0.051–0.286 ms. Median of per-seed latency speedups: 44.75x. At P95, candidate
0055 |   latency was 8.73–11.36 ms versus 10.67–15.67 ms for the control; the broad
0056 |   updates erase much of the tail advantage.
0057 | - Direct exact-ID boolean lookup averaged 0.060–0.064 microseconds over 10,000
0058 |   reads per seed. This is an array lookup, not a natural-language query.

0060 | OBSERVED — MEMORY AND COMPUTE COST
0061 | The full-recompute data layout was estimated at 837,080–840,128 bytes. The
0062 | candidate layout was estimated at 1,036,244–1,039,836 bytes, or 1.238x the
0063 | control, including a 199,164–199,708-byte reverse index. These are calculated
0064 | fixed-width struct sizes, not measured on-disk serialization. The nominal
0065 | 9-byte base-claim and 24-byte rule/source/time records are size-accounting
0066 | placeholders; source/time fields were not used by update semantics. The
0067 | algorithm stores supplied graph structure and truth values only. The estimates
0068 | exclude original prose, tokenizer/model parameters, vectors, semantic-query
0069 | indexes, operating-system and interpreter libraries, and language-generation
0070 | prompt/KV state. Raw generated JSON fixtures occupy 1,252,419 bytes total.
0071 | 
0072 | Peak process RSS was 46,096,384–46,178,304 bytes on Python 3.13.7 / Windows.
0073 | The experiment used CPU only: no training, GPU, external service or shared
0074 | accelerator. Physical inference VRAM was 0 bytes because no model was loaded;
0075 | this is not a VRAM-efficiency comparison. The initial experiment command took
0076 | 4.8 seconds by tool wall-clock observation. No training compute was used.
0077 | Graph generation plus reverse-index construction took 0.029–0.042 seconds per
0078 | seed; it was not separately timed or amortized against a long-life write
0079 | stream.
0080 | 
0081 | OBSERVED — CONTROL AND PROTOCOL
0082 | Strongest control: recompute the entire current closure in topological order
0083 | after every identical write, using the same fact values and rules. It is exact
0084 | for this acyclic rule family. The code compares every proposition after each
0085 | write, not only a sampled query list. Raw inputs and result JSON are preserved
0086 | in 01_raw/ and 01_results.json. The frozen design is in 00_PROTOCOL.txt.
0087 | 
0088 | The initial write schedule included 2–3 duplicate roots per seed. Those
0089 | repeated writes were idempotent and are disclosed above. No data were removed
0090 | or rewritten after the run. This screen has a hand-specified support graph;
0091 | there was no held-out language/domain transfer set, no hallucination test, and
0092 | no confidence/provenance calibration.
0093 | 
0094 | INFERRED — LIMITED INTERPRETATION
0095 | The reverse dependency index can reduce update work when edits have few
0096 | downstream consequences. The observed high-impact tail shows that broad edits
0097 | can still require near-global recomputation. Storage overhead in this generated
0098 | fixture was modest relative to the rule table, but true long-life memory must
0099 | also retain original evidence and semantic indexes, whose costs were not
0100 | measured. Exact parity establishes only the tested algorithm/data family.
0101 | 
0102 | The 10,284.5x median rule-check reduction is not a 10,284.5x total-work,
0103 | answer-speed or AI capability gain; it excludes reverse-index edge scans. The
0104 | measured 44.75x median update-latency ratio includes those scans but is highly
0105 | topology-dependent: 37.2% of writes touch only the source, while 26.7% touch
0106 | more than 5,000 nodes. P95 update times are close to full recomputation. The
0107 | answer operation was a direct boolean array lookup. The test does not show
0108 | that a language model can extract claims, maintain dependencies, resolve
0109 | source reliability, answer open-ended questions, or transfer.
0110 | 
0111 | PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTION / DECISION
0112 | 00_PROTOCOL.txt predicted exact state parity, at least 10x fewer rule checks
0113 | for sparse edits, and no more than 3x calculated persistent bytes. Those
0114 | nominal thresholds were met in the generated family. The work-count prediction
0115 | did not include index scans, the workload included seven idempotent writes,
0116 | and its impact distribution has a broad high-cost tail. Decision: OBSERVED
0117 | exact-propagation component; INCONCLUSIVE for the candidate as an AI advance.
0118 | Do not promote or scale this exact synthetic recipe unchanged.
0119 | 
0120 | STOPPING RULE
0121 | For any successor, stop if an exact reference state differs, any stale claim
0122 | survives, median work improvement is under 10x when no more than 5% of records
0123 | are affected, or total stored state exceeds 3x the matched full-recompute
0124 | control. A successor must add a changed mechanism or a language-grounded test;
0125 | do not rerun this exact graph/update schedule as a fresh study.

0188 | PROPOSED — CREDIBLE GENERAL-MODEL PATH
0189 | The next question is whether a language model can write into and use a revised
0190 | belief ledger without stale or unsupported answers. With language/hybrid and
0191 | evaluation lanes, define a preregistered multi-session test with human-readable
0192 | facts, source confidence, paraphrases, contradiction updates and multi-hop
0193 | questions. Compare the same local language model, tokenizer, decoding,
0194 | context/KV limit and call budget with (a) append-only text plus strong retrieval,
0195 | (b) topic-document consolidation, and (c) proof-dependency revision. Split by
0196 | entities/templates and hide all updated facts until the session writer sees
0197 | them. Measure extraction precision/recall, update correctness, answer accuracy,
0198 | stale-answer rate, abstention, per-answer tokens/tool calls/latency, persisted
0199 | original text and index bytes, host RAM and peak VRAM from cold load through
0200 | answer. Only if this passes can the lane claim language-grounded revisable
0201 | memory; a general model additionally needs broad conversation, reasoning and
0202 | transfer under matched resources.
