A03 — Compute-paced growth improved time to quality in a small byte LM

EVIDENCE STATUS: ONE-SEED CORRECTED CONFIRMATION; ESTABLISHED METHOD

Starting with fewer active blocks allowed more cheap updates before growing to the same final network.

RECIPE
Train with 4, then 8, then 12 blocks. Grow at one quarter and one half of the
estimated computation budget. Copy neighboring features, zero new output
projections, retain old optimizer states, and give new blocks independent
parameters. Pace the learning rate by computation rather than step count.

WHAT SUCCEEDED
The prospective corrected check used a 1,212,768-parameter byte-level
WikiText-2 model on two CPU threads, seed 5801. Reference/progressive:
  Timed wall:       152.942 / 90.666 seconds.
  Process CPU:      269.500 / 160.656 seconds.
  Updates:         1,150 / 1,410.
  Full-test CE:    1.9274640 / 1.8635314 nats/byte.
Snapshot-adjusted wall saving was 40.700%; CPU saving 40.373%.
The reference used its earliest single qualifying validation checkpoint;
the fully expanded candidate had to qualify twice consecutively and then stop.
Actual saved stopped checkpoints were evaluated on the full test split.

LIMITS AND HISTORY
This was one new prospective seed after a flawed original reference rule;
the original failed gates were preserved. Earlier three-seed growth screens
support the direction but are not three independent repeats of this corrected
time-to-quality protocol. Extra training examples are part of the mechanism.
The work proxy is not exact FLOPs. Warmup and research/calibration costs are
outside the quoted interval. No GPU, large-model or peak-memory gain was shown.
The more unusual temporary-cancellation variant did not consistently beat
simple growth and is not the promoted result.

HOW TO USE THIS
Grow the model only with explicit insertion correctness checks and a fixed
time-to-quality rule. Measure complete saved-model quality at the stop, not
the later final-budget model. Model growth itself has substantial prior art.
