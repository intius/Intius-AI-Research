A02 — Compilation cost dominated a short LoRA adaptation run

EVIDENCE STATUS: REPEATABLE ENGINEERING RESULT; ESTABLISHED METHOD

Packed scan execution reduced complete adaptation time even though its warmed update was slower.

WHAT WORKED
A Qwen3-0.6B model with rank-8 LoRA and 601,096,192 total stored values
completed 512 exact-objective updates in 111.694 seconds; a same-seed
fresh-process repeat took 111.251 seconds and reproduced checkpoints byte
for byte. The dense compiled reference took 266.449 seconds: a 2.390x
complete-run ratio. Warm scan updates were about 116 ms versus dense 74 ms.
The short-run benefit came from reduced compilation/setup, not faster steps.

QUALITY AND NUMERICAL LIMITS
Selected development scores were scan 60/64 and dense 61/64. Scan's language
guard CE increased 1.3104% versus its original backbone, within the 5% gate.
On frozen 256-problem confirmation: scan/dense primary 195/179 and reformulated
183/175. Paired p-values 0.07637 and 0.39612 did not establish population-level
accuracy superiority. The two renderings are the same 256 underlying problems.
Different selected epochs and numerical settings also limit attribution.
An eight-update parameter-replay tolerance failed: 6.0644e-5 versus 5e-5.
Thus scan and dense were not demonstrated bit-identical optimizer trajectories.

HOW TO USE THIS
For short adapter jobs, benchmark load, compilation, optimizer preparation,
training, saving and warm steps separately. Compare identical stopping rules
and actual saved quality. Do not extrapolate a short-job setup gain to long
pretraining. Scan packing is established execution engineering; it does not
compress away the 596M-parameter pretrained backbone.
